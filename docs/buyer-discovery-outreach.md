# Buyer discovery outreach kit

Status: **internal draft; not sent.** This kit prepares customer discovery. It does not establish demand, select a buyer, quote a price, or make a commitment on Martin's behalf.

## Claims to keep accurate

- Aequitas is a prototype for voluntary consumer-dispute workflows.
- The current case-agent path has passed CI using synthetic cases and a stub model. No live-model quality, real-case processing, buyer demand, or production operation has been established.
- The proposed paid pilot is a hypothesis for one program in one isolated deployment. There is no approved price, contract, or launch date.
- The proposed workflow aims to make evidence and consent traceable, give each party control over their own outcome, and hand exceptions to program staff. Do not promise a particular resolution, legal result, time saving, or reduction in staff.
- Do not ask anyone to share case files, party names, personal information, or confidential operating material.

Use only after Martin chooses the recipient and approves sending. Personalize the bracketed fields from public or already authorized information. Do not imply that the recipient is selected as a buyer.

## 20-minute conversation

1. **Purpose and boundaries (2 min):** confirm that the conversation is about process, not a live product demo or legal advice; do not record unless the participant explicitly agrees.
2. **Recent workflow (5 min):** ask the participant to describe three recent routine retail cases without identifying anyone. Where did each wait? What work did staff perform? Which cases were out of scope?
3. **Volume and effort (4 min):** ask for annual eligible volume and staff time per case. Mark every figure as observed, estimated, or unknown; note the period and source.
4. **Current alternatives and gap (3 min):** identify existing portals, PARLe or sector services, internal processes, and why any cases remain outside them.
5. **Buyer and safeguards (4 min):** identify the workflow owner, budget owner, procurement route, privacy/security review, independent review expectations, retention needs, and staff capacity for escalations.
6. **Specific next step (2 min):** ask whether a named person can review a non-confidential pilot outline or cost model. Record the exact action and owner only if they agree.

Do not ask participants to upload, forward, display, or describe identifiable case material. If sensitive details arise, steer back to process-level information and leave the details out of notes.

### Optional DSA Canada qualification addendum

Use only if Martin selects DSA Canada for discovery. This is an internal interview aid, not a prospect selection or sent invitation. DSA's public complaint page says complaints are rare, and its 2024 Code assigns complaint decisions to the independent Code Administrator ([complaint process](https://dsawebsite.gtacns.com/complaint-resolution/); [2024 Code](https://dsa.ca/assets/dsa/documents/Code%20of%20Ethics%202024/DSA-DSA%20Codes%20of%20Ethics_ENG_2024.pdf)). First establish whether there is enough eligible work to justify software at all.

1. **Volume:** Over the last 24 months, how many written complaints reached the Code Administrator? Please separate consumers, independent sales consultants, and other complaint types; distinguish matters screened out, resolved informally, and decided under the Code. What source supports each count?
2. **Current workflow:** From written complaint through the member or salesperson's opportunity to be heard and the Administrator's decision, which steps are manual, which tools are used, where do files wait, and how much staff effort does a typical case take?
3. **Decision authority:** Which routine steps could software support while preserving the Code Administrator's independence and final authority? Would a non-binding proposal followed by mutual consent fit any complaint type, or conflict with the current Code procedure?
4. **Party safeguards:** What access, correction, representation, confidentiality, conflict disclosure, appeal or review, retention, and deletion controls would be required for consumers and members or salespeople?
5. **Buyer and budget:** Who would own and pay for case-management software: the Association, the Code Administrator's office, or an individual member? Who controls the budget and procurement, and which privacy/security approvals apply? Supplier membership is not evidence of a software budget or purchase intent.
6. **Fit test:** What observed workflow gap would justify a pilot if complaints are rare? What evidence would rule the opportunity out as a buyer and leave only a possible channel or partnership?

Record only aggregate counts, role-level responsibility, and process-level effort. Do not request complaint files, party identifiers, member-specific allegations, or confidential Code Administrator material. Keep every answer labelled as observed, estimated, or unknown; an interview can establish problem evidence, but not buyer or pilot evidence without the required budget owner and concrete next step.

### Optional Manitoba Consumer Protection Office qualification addendum

Use only if Martin selects the Manitoba Consumer Protection Office for discovery. This is an internal interview aid, not a prospect selection or sent invitation. Manitoba's public process describes voluntary consumer-business complaint handling, while its vendor guidance says most service contracts are handled by departments and vendors may approach program end users. The current public guidance gives conflicting $75,000 and $100,000 service-tender summaries; confirm the applicable buyer, delegated authority and procurement path directly ([complaint process](https://gov.mb.ca/cp/cpo/complaint.html), [How Government Buys](https://www.gov.mb.ca/central/psc/buysell/index.html), [Procurement FAQ](https://gov.mb.ca/central/psc/faq.html)).

1. **Eligible volume and effort:** In the last 12 months, how many consumer-business matters were routine retail disputes that reached an officer? Separate inquiries, screened-out matters, investigations and voluntary resolutions. What records support each count, and what staff time does a typical eligible matter require?
2. **Current workflow and gap:** Which steps use existing case-management tools, and where do eligible matters wait or require repeated staff work? Which cases are already served by PARLe, another process or ordinary officer handling?
3. **Decision fit:** Could software support any clearly bounded routine step while an authorized officer retains the required decision and oversight? Would a non-binding proposal followed by party consent fit the Office's mandate and practice, or would it be unsuitable?
4. **Buyer and procurement:** Which program role owns the workflow, who controls an applicable budget, and which department or purchasing authority would contract? Would a pilot be a departmental purchase or a tender? Which current threshold, vendor terms, security review and procurement timeline apply?
5. **Privacy and operations:** Which privacy, security, records-access, retention/deletion, hosting-location and human-review requirements would need approval before a pilot? What share of cases would staff need to take back?
6. **Fit test:** What evidence would justify a small paid pilot, and what evidence would rule the Office out as a buyer? Identify the budget-owning role and a concrete next step only if the participant agrees.

### Evidence stages

- **Problem evidence:** a program operator describes a repeated workflow gap and supports eligible volume or staff effort with observed records or a clearly labeled estimate.
- **Buyer evidence:** a named budget-owning role confirms a plausible funding source, purchasing authority, and procurement path. Interest from a non-buyer is not buyer evidence.
- **Pilot evidence:** the organization agrees in writing to a defined next step toward a pilot, such as reviewing a scoped proposal with its budget owner and privacy/security reviewers. This is not a contract, purchase order, or permission to use case data.

Do not report demand as validated until evidence reaches the relevant stage. Keep public statistics, interview estimates, and confirmed procurement facts separate.
