# Buyer discovery outreach kit

Status: **internal draft; not sent.** This kit prepares customer discovery. It does not establish demand, select a buyer, quote a price, or make a commitment on Martin's behalf.

## Claims to keep accurate

- Aequitas is a prototype for voluntary consumer-dispute workflows.
- The current case-agent path has passed CI using synthetic cases and a stub model. No live-model quality, real-case processing, buyer demand, or production operation has been established.
- The proposed paid pilot is a hypothesis for one program in one isolated deployment. There is no approved price, contract, or launch date.
- The proposed workflow aims to make evidence and consent traceable, give each party control over their own outcome, and hand exceptions to program staff. Do not promise a particular resolution, legal result, time saving, or reduction in staff.
- Do not ask anyone to share case files, party names, personal information, or confidential operating material.

## Initial invitation

Use only after Martin chooses the recipient and approves sending. Personalize the bracketed fields from public or already authorized information. Do not imply that the recipient is selected as a buyer.

### English

**Subject:** 20-minute conversation about consumer complaint workflows

Hello [Name],

I’m researching how [organization/program] handles routine disputes between consumers and businesses, especially where the current process leaves staff assembling evidence or following up with both sides. I’m developing Aequitas, a bilingual case-workflow prototype, and want to understand the work before deciding whether a paid pilot would make sense.

The case-agent prototype has only been tested with synthetic cases and a stub model. It has not processed real cases, and I’m not asking you to share case files or confidential information. I’m hoping to learn about the workflow, approximate volumes and effort, existing alternatives, and the safeguards or procurement steps a program would require.

Would you be open to a 20-minute conversation? If another role owns this workflow or its budget, a pointer would also help. This is research, not a purchase request or a priced offer.

Thank you,
Martin

## 20-minute conversation

1. **Purpose and boundaries (2 min):** confirm that the conversation is about process, not a live product demo or legal advice; do not record unless the participant explicitly agrees.
2. **Recent workflow (5 min):** ask the participant to describe three recent routine retail cases without identifying anyone. Where did each wait? What work did staff perform? Which cases were out of scope?
3. **Volume and effort (4 min):** ask for annual eligible volume and staff time per case. Mark every figure as observed, estimated, or unknown; note the period and source.
4. **Current alternatives and gap (3 min):** identify existing portals, PARLe or sector services, internal processes, and why any cases remain outside them.
5. **Buyer and safeguards (4 min):** identify the workflow owner, budget owner, procurement route, privacy/security review, independent review expectations, retention needs, and staff capacity for escalations.
6. **Specific next step (2 min):** ask whether a named person can review a non-confidential pilot outline or cost model. Record the exact action and owner only if they agree.

Do not ask participants to upload, forward, display, or describe identifiable case material. If sensitive details arise, steer back to process-level information and leave the details out of notes.

### Evidence stages

- **Problem evidence:** a program operator describes a repeated workflow gap and supports eligible volume or staff effort with observed records or a clearly labeled estimate.
- **Buyer evidence:** a named budget-owning role confirms a plausible funding source, purchasing authority, and procurement path. Interest from a non-buyer is not buyer evidence.
- **Pilot evidence:** the organization agrees in writing to a defined next step toward a pilot, such as reviewing a scoped proposal with its budget owner and privacy/security reviewers. This is not a contract, purchase order, or permission to use case data.

Do not report demand as validated until evidence reaches the relevant stage. Keep public statistics, interview estimates, and confirmed procurement facts separate.

## Stop conditions

- Stop the sales discussion if the recipient expects the prototype to adjudicate, provide legal advice, guarantee settlements, or eliminate human review.
- Do not quote until eligible case volume, staff baseline, provider and hosting costs, support expectations, procurement path, and safeguards are understood.
- Do not treat an interview, positive reaction, or request for more information as a purchase commitment.
- Do not send this kit, contact a prospect, or share product materials externally without Martin's explicit direction.
