## Workflow problem (hypothesis)

Staff assemble each file from email, attachments and phone notes. Parties cannot see a dated record of what they submitted, and the written proposal is hard to trace to evidence. Consent is not tied to a specific version of what the party read.

## Proposed pilot offer

- One program, one isolated deployment, `RETAIL`, two-party cases, fr-CA and en-CA.
- The agent handles the configured routine path without a case manager. Before evidence extraction or provider processing, each party must accept the current bilingual notice identifying the provider, account/project, model and prompt version; a missing choice returns the case to staff. Each party then sees fixed bilingual proposal wording, can consent or decline, can ask for a person, and keeps control over their own evidence and outcome. Staff can trace each proposal-basis claim to a verified extracted excerpt; that link is review support, not proof the excerpt semantically supports the remedy. Independent approver sign-off remains required unless the program explicitly enables consent-only resolution after owner and governance approval.
- Program staff remain available for every exception, request for review, decline, or case outside scope. Human-managed cases retain the separate approver step.
- The agent remains off until the program rules, provider account and data terms, configured bilingual processing notices, oversight, privacy/legal review, and live synthetic evaluation are resolved. No AI call is enabled by default.
- Price, term and payment: **not set.** No billing is built.

## Commercial packaging and price logic (internal hypothesis; no price approved)

**Buyer and payer hypothesis:** an independent dispute-resolution program or member organization with a documented routine retail case queue that its current service does not handle. The program pays; neither party to the dispute pays a fee to use Aequitas. This is a positioning hypothesis, not validated demand. Québec already offers PARLe as a free consumer–merchant negotiation service, and a government small-claims ODR platform is active, so the first prospect should be outside those covered workflows rather than pitched as their replacement ([OPC PARLe](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/parle-commercant/), [Québec ODR project dashboard](https://www.tableaudebordprojetsri.gouv.qc.ca/tableau-de-bord/projet/29637912)). A Canadian analogue, OBSI, provides consumer dispute resolution free to consumers while participating firms fund the service under regulatory requirements and independence safeguards; that supports the payer model as an analogy, not as a direct price comparison ([OBSI funding model](https://www.obsi.ca/en/for-consumers/how-were-funded/)).

**SKU 1 — paid pilot:** one program, one isolated deployment, bilingual fr-CA/en-CA, two-party retail cases, configured eligibility limits, staff onboarding, and a time-limited evaluation with an agreed case cap and service measures. The owner, operator and counsel gates still apply; no real case data enters the pilot before those gates are resolved. Quote a fixed implementation fee plus a fixed pilot fee so the buyer can budget the deployment and evaluation. The minimum quote must cover setup labor, hosting, storage, email, provider usage at the agreed cap, and staff support. Compare it with the buyer's observed staff cost for eligible cases; do not tie payment to consent, settlement or case outcome. No amount is set until discovery establishes the buyer's baseline costs, case volume, support needs, and procurement path.

**SKU 2 — continued service, conditional on pilot evidence:** an annual program licence with an included case-volume band and separately priced managed operations or integrations. Set the volume bands and renewal price from observed usage, hosting/support costs, and the program's own baseline; do not publish a price ladder yet.

**Promise boundary and exclusions:** Aequitas offers workflow software and a non-binding proposal process, not adjudication, legal advice, guaranteed settlement, or a promised reduction in case time. The pilot does not include multiple programs, multi-tenant hosting, custom integrations, legal review, or unlimited human case-management capacity. Do not tie payment to consent, settlement, or the outcome of a dispute.

**Discovery inputs required before quoting:** eligible cases per year; current staff minutes and cost per case; existing software and unresolved workflow; human-review capacity; hosting and support expectations; who controls the budget and procurement; whether both parties trust a program-funded service; and the provider/privacy terms the program will accept. Until these are known, the offer is shaped but not priced.

## Pilot scorecard

Use the program's pre-pilot data for comparable cases as the baseline. The operator report now includes two separate cohorts: (1) intake opened in the selected UTC window, for the existing intake-to-first-issue timing; and (2) the case agent was enrolled in the selected UTC window, with lifecycle outcomes read through the exclusive end date. For the intake cohort it reports how many had and had not received an issue by the report end; the latter can include cases that already closed or were handed off, so it is not described as “still awaiting issue.” The agent-enrollment cohort reports aggregate counts for agent-origin proposals, handoffs, agent-origin closure without handoff, staff closure without a recorded handoff, closure after handoff, and cases still with the agent or awaiting staff after handoff. It also reports median and 90th-percentile hours from enrollment to an agent proposal and from enrollment to agent-origin closure.

After an approved pilot has non-simulated agent-managed cases, an operator can produce the aggregates with `npm run agent:metrics -- --from=YYYY-MM-DD --to=YYYY-MM-DD`. Dates are UTC calendar days; `--to` is exclusive. Both cohorts select their opening event in the chosen window and read later lifecycle events before its end date. The JSON omits case identifiers, party data and evidence, and excludes synthetic cases. “Closed by agent without handoff” is an event-log measure; it does not mean that no human approval occurred, since independent approver sign-off may still be required. Small counts are descriptive, and the command does not establish causation or compare against a baseline automatically.

## What works end to end (synthetic data, disposable database)

The manual journey is covered: submission → scanned evidence → positions → case manager writes a cited recommendation without AI → issuance → per-party outcomes → independent approver sign-off → version-bound party consent → export and closure. The autonomous routine path is also covered with synthetic data and a stub model: agent intake → respondent invitation → both parties' evidence and positions → each party accepts the current provider-processing notice → exact evidence citations and fixed remedy wording → both consents → export and closure. A no-choice case waits without extracting evidence and returns to staff at the deadline. Neither journey proves live-model quality or readiness for real cases. See `test/integration/authored-draft.test.ts`, `test/integration/case-agent-routine.test.ts`, and the corresponding browser tests.

## Launch gates (policy or manual; not decided in code)

1. **Release and hosting decision.** Confirm the deployed commit and host, and choose the pilot deployment. Merging into `main` may trigger Railway.
2. **Applicable privacy regime and data flow.** Counsel and the privacy officer must identify the responsible organization and provider roles. No Aequitas deployment-specific assessment or written transfer agreement is recorded here. If the Québec private-sector Act applies, its s. 3.3 addresses information-system projects involving personal information; s. 17 addresses out-of-Québec communications and outsourced processing, including an assessment, adequate protection and written agreement. Map the actual model, hosting, email, storage and backup locations before live data. The Act excludes public bodies and information held on their behalf, so confirm the regime for the selected program ([current Act](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/p-39.1); [CAI guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/utilisation-communication-renseignements-personnels)).
   - **Provider location:** the public terms reviewed for the supported Gemini and Workers AI routes do not establish Québec- or Canada-only inference. If the program requires that location, obtain written account-specific processing-location and retention commitments and counsel review before sending real case data; see the [provider findings](case-agent-prototype.md#provider-data-handling-rechecked-2026-11-07).
   - **Vertex candidate:** code now supports Google Cloud `gemini-3.5-flash` at `northamerica-northeast1`, which its model page lists for Canada ML processing. This is not a production provider approval: regional endpoints alone do not guarantee in-region processing, and project-level prompt logging, caching, current terms, data at rest, and other infrastructure locations remain unverified. 3. **Automated decision and human review.** Counsel must decide whether the proposal-and-consent workflow, including the optional consent-only resolution setting, is a decision based exclusively on automated processing under s. 12.1, and specify any notice, explanation, correction and staff-review process. The application offers a request-for-person path before closure; its legal sufficiency and timing are unreviewed ([current Act, s. 12.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/p-39.1); [CAI guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/utilisation-communication-renseignements-personnels)).
4. **Retention, access and deletion.** No retention or deletion service exists. Events are append-only by database trigger, so erasure needs a design (for example, separating personal fields from the hash chain). The source-bounded inventory and target-design draft is in [`docs/retention-deletion-design.md`](retention-deletion-design.md); it sets no periods and is not legal approval. If the Act applies, s. 11 requires at least one year of retention for personal information used to make a decision; s. 3.2 requires governance practices for retention/destruction, lifecycle roles and complaints. Counsel and the program must determine which records qualify, set the schedule and deletion-response design, and confirm party access in a structured, commonly used format. No periods are set here ([current Act, ss. 3.2, 3.3 and 11](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/p-39.1)).
5. **Organization isolation.** Roles are global; any case manager sees unclaimed open cases; admins are global. Multi-organization use is blocked. One program per deployment.
6. **Identity and access.** Real passkey enrolment on the production hostname; identity check before re-enrolling the six existing users.
7. **Email.** Delivery through the configured provider and the retry job must be checked on the pilot host.
8. **Party-facing wording.** Counsel review of collection notices, consent statements, the exact provider/model processing notice in both locales, outcome text guidance, and the notice that withholds the shared recommendation.
9. **Commercial.** Pricing, contract, data processing terms.
