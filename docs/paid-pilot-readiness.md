## Buyer (hypothesis, not validated demand)

A Québec organization that runs voluntary consumer dispute resolution between a buyer and a seller. The proposed agent handles eligible routine `RETAIL` cases; program staff take exceptions and cases outside scope. No customer conversation, letter of intent or pricing evidence is in this repository.

The primary pilot context remains Québec. Manitoba's Consumer Protection Office is a newly surfaced discovery lead because its public service handles consumer-business disputes, but it is not a selected prospect; its public-body mandate, jurisdiction and procurement fit require separate review.

**Additional private-sector discovery candidate — Direct Sellers Association of Canada (DSA Canada; not a validated buyer):** DSA Canada's public complaint process is a closer operating analogue than a generic contact centre. A consumer first tries to resolve the matter with a member company or independent sales consultant, then can submit a written complaint to the independent Code Administrator; the public page says complaints are rare and that member companies agree to honour the Administrator's decisions ([DSA complaint process](https://dsawebsite.gtacns.com/complaint-resolution/)). The Association's 2024 Code says the Administrator hears and determines complaints, gives the member or salesperson an opportunity to be heard fully, and decides whether a Code violation occurred ([DSA Code of Ethics and Business Practices, 2024](https://dsa.ca/assets/dsa/documents/Code%20of%20Ethics%202024/DSA-DSA%20Codes%20of%20Ethics_ENG_2024.pdf)). This is only a partial product fit: the process covers direct-selling conduct and can involve independent sales consultants, rather than ordinary store retail cases. No public evidence found here establishes complaint volume, staff burden, a digital-workflow gap, software budget, procurement route, or willingness to buy. Treat DSA Canada as a possible discovery or channel conversation, not a selected pilot or demand evidence. If Martin selects it for discovery, first ask for aggregate case counts, intake-to-decision time, staff effort, the software decision-maker, and whether the Code Administrator would retain the final decision. DSA Canada also offers supplier membership to service providers, but membership would only create an industry channel; it is not a customer commitment ([DSA membership categories](https://dsawebsite.gtacns.com/become-a-member/)).

**Competitive evidence checked 2026-11-06 (public sources only):**

- The Office de la protection du consommateur describes PARLe as a free online negotiation service for consumers and participating merchants, with access to an accredited external mediator when needed. Its merchant page reports several hundred participating merchants, about three quarters of files ending in agreement, an average duration of about five weeks, and 82% overall satisfaction. These are OPC-published service figures, not an independent comparison with Aequitas ([OPC: PARLe for merchants](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/parle-commercant)).
- The OPC's 2024–2025 annual report records 1,844 agreements on PARLe during that fiscal year. It also reports survey results for the value of settled disputes; those figures do not establish a market size or willingness to pay for Aequitas ([OPC: 2024–2025 annual report](https://cdn.opc.gouv.qc.ca/media/documents/a-propos/publication/RAG_2024-2025.pdf)).
- A Québec government project dashboard, updated 2026-10-01, lists an active Justice-sector online dispute resolution platform for disputes within the jurisdiction of the Court of Québec's Small Claims Division. The dashboard shows 45% progress, an estimated cost of $8,682,100, and an estimated end date of 2028-01-18 ([Québec project dashboard](https://www.tableaudebordprojetsri.gouv.qc.ca/tableau-de-bord/projet/29637912)). This is a material adjacent initiative; the public record does not establish its procurement needs, technical design, or whether it will cover Aequitas's exact routine workflow.

**Opportunity ranking checked 2026-11-06 (public evidence only; no demand validation):**

1. **Manitoba Consumer Protection Office — strongest named discovery lead; not a validated buyer.** The Office hears, mediates and investigates consumer complaints; its current public process describes an officer contacting the business and continuing until agreement or impasse, with participation voluntary and no imposed settlement. The 2024–25 annual report records 15,127 intake calls, emails or inquiries, 544 investigated complaints, 166 inspections or audits, and about $183,433 in consumer settlements or adjustments. These are office-wide figures across its broad mandate; the report does not identify eligible routine retail files, staff cost per file, a software budget, unmet need, or willingness to buy. This is a provincial public body operating under a different legal, privacy and procurement context from the Québec-oriented candidate; use it for discovery only, not as the selected pilot or evidence of procurement ([Manitoba CPO about](https://www.gov.mb.ca/cp/cpo/about.html), [complaint-handling process](https://gov.mb.ca/cp/cpo/complaint.html), [2024–25 Public Service Delivery annual report, pp. 64–65](https://www.gov.mb.ca/asset_library/en/annualreports/2024_2025/annual-report-psd-24-25.pdf)).
2. **An independent Québec dispute-resolution program with a documented retail queue outside existing services — best product and jurisdiction fit, weak demand evidence.** Its routine two-party files most closely match the product, and an operator could be the buyer while both parties remain free. No named organization, unmet workflow, case volume, software budget, or willingness to pilot is evidenced here. The segment remains a hypothesis, not a lead or market-size claim.
3. **Saskatchewan FCAA Consumer Rights — benchmark or possible integration conversation, not a near-term sales lead.** Saskatchewan already operates a free online consumer-dispute path that invites the business and can refer a case to an independent mediator. The public evidence does not show a procurement need or that Aequitas would improve the existing service ([FCAA Consumer Rights](https://fcaa.gov.sk.ca/consumer-rights), [how the service works](https://fcaa.gov.sk.ca/consumer-rights-copy/how-to-use-the-consumer-rights-services)).
4. **BBB's national or regional complaint-handling operation — identifiable but not an evident gap.** BBB already takes marketplace disputes through an online complaint process, asks businesses to respond within 10–15 days, generally closes complaints within about 30 days, and may offer mediation or arbitration depending on region. The Better Business Bureau of Canada’s Northern Capital Regions and Quebec was expelled from the BBB system in June 2024; BBB says the region continues to receive service from other member BBBs and that inquiries are handled across North America. This is a possible operator conversation only if it reveals an unmet internal workflow; the published process is not evidence that BBB needs Aequitas ([BBB complaint process](https://www.bbb.org/process-of-complaints-and-reviews/complaints), [BBB dispute handling](https://www.bbb.org/about/bbb-dispute-handling-and-resolution), [BBB Quebec service change](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/30453-international-association-of-better-business-bureaus-expels-member-bbb)).
5. **OPC/PARLe or Québec's public ODR work — strong topical overlap, weak first-customer fit.** PARLe is a free, private online negotiation platform for consumers and participating merchants; parties can upload evidence and use an accredited mediator if needed. The OPC also says it cannot intervene in every reported situation and leaves consumers responsible for negotiating. That establishes both a close free alternative and a possible service gap, but not a separate funded buyer, unmet volume, or procurement path. Do not pitch Aequitas as a replacement for PARLe or the government's active small-claims ODR project ([OPC on PARLe](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/consommateur/probleme-commercant/ressources/parle-consommation), [OPC complaint role](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/consumer/resolve-problem/steps), [Québec ODR project](https://www.tableaudebordprojetsri.gouv.qc.ca/tableau-de-bord/projet/29637912)).
6. **Sector complaint bodies — exclude from the retail MVP.** CCTS handles telecom and television complaints; AMF's complaint and mediation path is for financial-sector matters. These show that some Canadian redress programs are mandate-specific, not evidence that either body is a buyer for Aequitas's retail product ([CCTS mandate](https://www.ccts-cprst.ca/for-consumers/faq/), [AMF complaint process](https://lautorite.qc.ca/en/general-public/assistance-and-complaints/making-a-complaint)).
7. **A single merchant or marketplace as payer — low priority until neutrality is proven.** The product's fairness promise depends on both parties trusting the process. One party funding the service could create a perceived conflict; this is a product-governance concern, not evidence about buyer demand.

**Additional retail-program benchmark:** The Retail Council of Canada's Scanner Price Accuracy Code has a defined consumer complaint path: store staff and managers first, then a designated company representative, then an industry committee, with optional arbitration on a cost-recovery basis. Its scope is narrow, it is already an established industry process, and Quebec has a separate regulated price-accuracy regime. Treat it as a process benchmark or exploratory conversation only; current public evidence does not establish an Aequitas fit, unmet need, or buyer budget ([RCC Scanner Price Accuracy Code](https://www.retailcouncil.org/scanner-price-accuracy-code/), [consumer FAQ](https://www.retailcouncil.org/scanner-price-accuracy-code/scanner-price-accuracy-code-frequently-asked-questions-by-consumers/)).

**Commercial implication:** the strongest public-sector queue signal found is Manitoba, while Québec remains the better product-context fit. Neither is validated as a buyer. Compare them in discovery rather than changing the MVP jurisdiction from public web evidence alone. Do not pitch Aequitas as a replacement for PARLe or assume the OPC or Manitoba CPO is a buyer. Before pricing or making differentiator claims, speak with operators: review recent files and current tools, quantify eligible volume and staff effort, identify the budget/procurement owner, understand why existing services do not fit, and confirm both parties' trust and required safeguards. A stated interest is not purchase evidence; seek a named budget owner and written next step.

**Initial discovery guide (3–5 operator interviews; directional, not statistically representative):**

- Walk through the last three routine retail cases from intake to closure. Where did each case wait, need staff time, or leave the parties uncertain?
- Which cases are already handled by PARLe, a sector service, an existing online portal, an internal process, or small claims? What cases are left out, and how often?
- Roughly how many cases per year fit Aequitas's narrow `RETAIL`, two-party, configured-limit path? What share would be escalated under the current rules?
- What tools hold the case narrative, evidence, party messages, decisions, and audit history today? What does staff spend per file in minutes or cost?
- Who owns the workflow, software budget and procurement decision? What current budget line could pay for a pilot, and which security/privacy review is required?
- What independent review, party notice, appeal, retention and privacy safeguards would be mandatory before using a non-binding machine-generated proposal?
- If the workflow gap and safeguards are credible, what specific next step could the organization commit to (for example, a named budget-owner meeting or a written evaluation plan)?

Do not collect case files, party identifiers, or confidential operational details for this discovery. Record who answered each question, whether a number is observed or estimated, the supporting source, and what remains unknown. An expression of interest without an identified workflow, budget owner and concrete next step does not validate demand.

The bilingual invitation, interview record, evidence-stage rubric, and follow-up drafts are in [docs/buyer-discovery-outreach.md](buyer-discovery-outreach.md). They are internal drafts and have not been sent.

## Workflow problem (hypothesis)

Staff assemble each file from email, attachments and phone notes. Parties cannot see a dated record of what they submitted, and the written proposal is hard to trace to evidence. Consent is not tied to a specific version of what the party read.

## Proposed pilot offer

- One program, one isolated deployment, `RETAIL`, two-party cases, fr-CA and en-CA.
- The agent handles the configured routine path without a case manager. Each party sees fixed bilingual proposal wording, can consent or decline, can ask for a person, and keeps control over their own evidence and outcome. Independent approver sign-off remains required unless the program explicitly enables consent-only resolution after owner and governance approval.
- Program staff remain available for every exception, request for review, decline, or case outside scope. Human-managed cases retain the separate approver step.
- The agent remains off until the program rules, provider account and data terms, oversight, privacy/legal review, and live synthetic evaluation are resolved. No AI call is enabled by default.
- Price, term and payment: **not set.** No billing is built.

## Commercial packaging and price logic (internal hypothesis; no price approved)

**Buyer and payer hypothesis:** an independent dispute-resolution program or member organization with a documented routine retail case queue that its current service does not handle. The program pays; neither party to the dispute pays a fee to use Aequitas. This is a positioning hypothesis, not validated demand. Québec already offers PARLe as a free consumer–merchant negotiation service, and a government small-claims ODR platform is active, so the first prospect should be outside those covered workflows rather than pitched as their replacement ([OPC PARLe](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/parle-commercant/), [Québec ODR project dashboard](https://www.tableaudebordprojetsri.gouv.qc.ca/tableau-de-bord/projet/29637912)). A Canadian analogue, OBSI, provides consumer dispute resolution free to consumers while participating firms fund the service under regulatory requirements and independence safeguards; that supports the payer model as an analogy, not as a direct price comparison ([OBSI funding model](https://www.obsi.ca/en/for-consumers/how-were-funded/)).

**SKU 1 — paid pilot:** one program, one isolated deployment, bilingual fr-CA/en-CA, two-party retail cases, configured eligibility limits, staff onboarding, and a time-limited evaluation with an agreed case cap and service measures. The owner, operator and counsel gates still apply; no real case data enters the pilot before those gates are resolved. Quote a fixed implementation fee plus a fixed pilot fee so the buyer can budget the deployment and evaluation. The minimum quote must cover setup labor, hosting, storage, email, provider usage at the agreed cap, and staff support. Compare it with the buyer's observed staff cost for eligible cases; do not tie payment to consent, settlement or case outcome. No amount is set until discovery establishes the buyer's baseline costs, case volume, support needs, and procurement path.

**SKU 2 — continued service, conditional on pilot evidence:** an annual program licence with an included case-volume band and separately priced managed operations or integrations. Set the volume bands and renewal price from observed usage, hosting/support costs, and the program's own baseline; do not publish a price ladder yet.

**Promise boundary and exclusions:** Aequitas offers workflow software and a non-binding proposal process, not adjudication, legal advice, guaranteed settlement, or a promised reduction in case time. The pilot does not include multiple programs, multi-tenant hosting, custom integrations, legal review, or unlimited human case-management capacity. Do not tie payment to consent, settlement, or the outcome of a dispute.

**Discovery inputs required before quoting:** eligible cases per year; current staff minutes and cost per case; existing software and unresolved workflow; human-review capacity; hosting and support expectations; who controls the budget and procurement; whether both parties trust a program-funded service; and the provider/privacy terms the program will accept. Until these are known, the offer is shaped but not priced.

## One measurable outcome

Median elapsed time from `EVIDENCE_INTAKE_OPENED` to `RECOMMENDATION_ISSUED`, read from the event log, against the program's own pre-pilot figure for comparable files. The program supplies the baseline. No target is set here.

After an approved pilot has non-simulated agent-managed cases, an operator can produce this aggregate with `npm run agent:metrics -- --from=YYYY-MM-DD --to=YYYY-MM-DD`. Dates are UTC calendar days; `--to` is exclusive. The report includes the number of cases entering intake, the number issued within the window, cases still awaiting issue, median and 90th-percentile hours. It omits case identifiers, party data and evidence, and excludes synthetic cases. Small counts remain descriptive; the command does not establish causation or compare against a baseline automatically.

## What works end to end (synthetic data, disposable database)

The manual journey is covered: submission → scanned evidence → positions → case manager writes a cited recommendation without AI → issuance → per-party outcomes → independent approver sign-off → version-bound party consent → export and closure. The autonomous routine path is also covered with synthetic data and a stub model: agent intake → respondent invitation → both parties' evidence and positions → exact evidence citations and fixed remedy wording → both consents → export and closure. Neither journey proves live-model quality or readiness for real cases. See `test/integration/authored-draft.test.ts`, `test/integration/case-agent-routine.test.ts`, and the corresponding browser tests.

## Onboarding steps

1. Martin chooses the release commit and deploys it to the single pilot host. Not done here.
2. Run `npx prisma migrate deploy` to apply all pending schema migrations on the selected release, including notification attempts, case-agent scheduler fields and the daily aggregate model-call counter.
3. Invite the program's case managers (`/admin/invites` or `scripts/issue-invite.ts`); each enrols a passkey on the production hostname.
4. Grant approver to one staff member who will not manage the same cases (`scripts/grant-approver.ts`).
5. Publish current consent statements in fr-CA and en-CA.
6. Set the program's explicit amount limit, daily UTC model-call ceiling, intake and response periods, respondent-withdrawal rule, and consent-only resolution policy. The daily ceiling limits call volume, not provider dollars; set provider-side spend limits or alerts separately. Keep `AEQUITAS_CASE_AGENT_CONSENT_ONLY_RESOLUTION=false` unless the owner explicitly approves waiving independent sign-off. Review the selected provider's current terms and set `AEQUITAS_AI_PROVIDER_TERMS_REVIEWED_FOR` to the exact identity `gemini:<project-id>:<model>` or `workers-ai:<account-id>:<model>`. For Gemini, also set `GEMINI_PROJECT_ID` and `AEQUITAS_GEMINI_PAID_TIER_CONFIRMED_FOR` to the verified API-key project ID. Run and manually review the bounded synthetic evaluation; set `AEQUITAS_CASE_AGENT_EVALUATION_REVIEWED_FOR` to `<provider-identity>:prompt:<AGENT_PROMPT_VERSION>` only after a successful review. Any provider, account/project, model or prompt-version change blocks the runtime until re-evaluated. The app cannot verify that a Gemini API key belongs to the declared project. Start the CT920 scheduled profile so failed notifications retry every 15 minutes and the case agent runs every 10 minutes. Confirm both operations report healthy with `npm run agent:status`; new cases remain unprocessed until complete program settings and all approval identities are present.
7. Set `AEQUITAS_PILOT_CONTACT_URL` at build time if the landing page should show the pilot contact.
8. Run one synthetic case end to end with program staff before any real file.

The automated case agent is the proposed routine workflow, but is off by default. Its program settings have no defaults; it needs the owner, operator and counsel decisions in `docs/case-agent-prototype.md` before any real case.

## Launch gates (policy or manual; not decided in code)

1. **Release and hosting decision.** Confirm the deployed commit and host, and choose the pilot deployment. Merging into `main` may trigger Railway.
2. **Applicable privacy regime and data flow.** Counsel and the privacy officer must identify the responsible organization and provider roles. No Aequitas deployment-specific assessment or written transfer agreement is recorded here. If the Québec private-sector Act applies, its s. 3.3 addresses information-system projects involving personal information; s. 17 addresses out-of-Québec communications and outsourced processing, including an assessment, adequate protection and written agreement. Map the actual model, hosting, email, storage and backup locations before live data. The Act excludes public bodies and information held on their behalf, so confirm the regime for the selected program ([current Act](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/p-39.1); [CAI guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/utilisation-communication-renseignements-personnels)).
3. **Automated decision and human review.** Counsel must decide whether the proposal-and-consent workflow, including the optional consent-only resolution setting, is a decision based exclusively on automated processing under s. 12.1, and specify any notice, explanation, correction and staff-review process. The application offers a request-for-person path before closure; its legal sufficiency and timing are unreviewed ([current Act, s. 12.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/p-39.1); [CAI guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/utilisation-communication-renseignements-personnels)).
4. **Retention, access and deletion.** No retention or deletion service exists. Events are append-only by database trigger, so erasure needs a design (for example, separating personal fields from the hash chain). If the Act applies, s. 11 requires at least one year of retention for personal information used to make a decision; s. 3.2 requires governance practices for retention/destruction, lifecycle roles and complaints. Counsel and the program must determine which records qualify, set the schedule and deletion-response design, and confirm party access in a structured, commonly used format. No periods are set here ([current Act, ss. 3.2, 3.3 and 11](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/p-39.1)).
5. **Organization isolation.** Roles are global; any case manager sees unclaimed open cases; admins are global. Multi-organization use is blocked. One program per deployment.
6. **Identity and access.** Real passkey enrolment on the production hostname; identity check before re-enrolling the six existing users.
7. **Email.** Delivery through the configured provider and the retry job must be checked on the pilot host.
8. **Party-facing wording.** Counsel review of collection notices, consent statements, outcome text guidance and the notice that withholds the shared recommendation.
9. **Commercial.** Pricing, contract, data processing terms.

## Known defects and limits

- Two parties only. No translation-review workflow.
- Invitation emails are sent directly (`src/lib/mail.ts`) with one immediate retry using the same provider idempotency key; if delivery remains uncertain, the active link is preserved and staff must resolve it. Account-recovery emails remain outside the retried outbox.
- Storage cleanup after a crashed write is manual.
